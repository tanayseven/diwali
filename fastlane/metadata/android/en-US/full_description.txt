Celebrate Diwali on the porch of a house at night, lit only by what you light on it, while fireworks burst over the city behind it.

<b>Diyas</b>: drag oil lamps onto the porch, put a wick and oil in each, and light them with a matchstick held to the wick. Tap a lit diya to turn it.

<b>Fireworks</b>: set out anars that throw up fountains of sparks, laddis of crackers that go off one after another, chakris that spin across the floor, sutli bombs that go off with a boom, and rockets that whoosh up out of their bottles and burst into stars. Light their fuses with the matchstick.

<b>Craft Kandil</b>: make akash kandils, the paper lanterns of Diwali, and hang them on the house, where they sway in the breeze.

<b>Decorate House</b>: hang strings of fairy lights across the house, in one colour or mixed, and watch them twinkle.

<b>Draw Rangoli</b>: draw on the porch floor in coloured powder.

<b>Photos</b>: take a photo of your porch and share it.

Every sound is heard from where it is, so headphones or stereo speakers are best. Background music plays throughout, with a volume control.

The game is in 22 languages: Assamese, Bengali, Dogri, English, Gujarati, Hindi, Hinglish, Kannada, Kashmiri, Konkani, Maithili, Malayalam, Manipuri, Marathi, Odia, Punjabi, Sanskrit, Santali, Sindhi, Tamil, Telugu and Urdu.

It collects nothing and needs no internet connection.

The game has flashing and twinkling lights, so it opens with a warning for people with photosensitive epilepsy.
